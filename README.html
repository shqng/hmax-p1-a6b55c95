HMAX Energy Management

Phase 1 concept prototype — traction energy. This is a design prototype built to test structure, workflow and role interaction. It is not production software, and the data in it is representative rather than real.

To open it

Double-click Start HMAX prototype.bat. It opens in your default browser. Leave the small black window open while you are using it, and close it when you are done.

If that does not work, open index.html directly in Chrome or Edge. Everything runs locally — nothing is sent anywhere, and no installation is required.

Start here

The first screen asks who you are. That choice is the point of the prototype — the product shows a different depth, a different home screen and a different set of actions depending on the role, because each role is accountable for something different.

RoleWhat they do, and where they land
Energy Performance Manager Owns performance and prioritisation. Decides what gets investigated and confirms whether a saving held up. Opens on the highest-level view in the product.
Energy Performance Analyst Builds and evidences the case. The heaviest daily user, and the role most of the design effort went into.
Operations / Timetable Manager Rules on operational cause, and holds the running-time and capacity budget that a change spends. Opens on decisions waiting for them.
Rolling Stock Engineer Rules on technical cause. Works at unit level — the deepest view in the product.

A ten-minute path through it

If you only have a short time, this route covers the structure without needing any background.

  1. Sign in as the Energy Performance Manager and look at the portfolio view. It is written as a position to be read rather than a dashboard to be operated.
  2. Switch role to the Analyst using the control in the top bar. Watch the strip that appears — a role change is treated as a handover, and the product states what has been passed and what that desk is accountable for.
  3. Open the case queue. It is grouped by what is blocking each item rather than by status.
  4. Open EC-2043 and read across the five steps — readiness, evidence, scenario, decision, result. Anything the system generated is marked and traceable back to the service that produced it.
  5. Switch to Operations and look at the same case at the decision step. The case is there because of what the change costs, and it is phrased in the units that role manages.
  6. Switch back to the Manager and open a closed case to see the result — what was expected against what was actually measured.

Things worth knowing

The data is representative. Figures, names and cases are illustrative and sized to be plausible. They are there to make the workflow legible, not to be analysed.

Not everything is clickable. It is a design prototype, so the paths described above are the ones that are built out. Other areas render but are not interactive.

Screens vary by role on purpose. If a view looks sparse, that is usually the intended depth for that role rather than a gap.

Your role selection is remembered in the browser. To start over, return to index.html.

What this is exploring

The current Green CBTC tooling shows traction energy performance well. What it does not do is guide someone to what matters first, make responsibility explicit, or connect an insight to a decision and then to a measured result.

This prototype tests one answer to that: a single case record that moves between roles, with an automated layer that prepares the analysis and a named person who makes each call. The division between those two — what the platform prepares, and what a person has to decide — is the main thing we are trying to get right.
